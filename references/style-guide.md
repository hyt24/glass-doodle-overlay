# Visual style guide

## Fixed poster composition

- Make every source image into one standalone 3:4 vertical poster. Never merge multiple uploaded photos into one output.
- Split the composition exactly 1:1 by height: the upper half is the lightly graded original photograph; the lower half is the glass-doodle version of the same scene.
- Keep both panels visually aligned to the same source world and preserve the main subject in each. Use only natural background expansion to fit the fixed crop.
- Keep the upper panel photographic and restrained: subtle exhibition-print color grading is welcome; stylization, illustrated texture, artificial lens effects, or a changed subject are not.

## Scene preservation

- Treat the photo as a documentary base. Preserve real geometry, background, focal subject, lighting direction, exposure, and composition.
- Frame the effect as an indoor photographer looking outward through a real transparent pane of glass. Maintain a clear outdoor view.
- Allow only a very slight glass haze or reflection. Never create heavy fog, condensation, droplets, frost, steam, diffuse blur, milky translucency, or a frosted-glass panel.
- When a busy background competes with the doodle, reduce only its saturation or sharpness very gently; never soften or recolor the main subject.

## Doodle language

- Use opaque white with occasional imperfect stroke variation, as if drawn directly onto glass with a broad white paint marker, wax crayon, or window marker.
- Prefer 3–10 broad strokes and sparse negative space. Keep the line ends blunt, slightly uneven, and clearly handmade.
- Follow and extend the existing contour to reveal the imagined form. Do not cover or redraw the actual object in solid paint.
- Keep the doodle visually in the foreground. It may overlap the real subject in the 2D image, but must feel physically separated by the glass plane.

## Mood and exclusions

- Aim for natural, dreamy, cute, and serendipitous—an amusing shape noticed through a window and casually traced in the moment.
- Avoid a UI mockup, sticker sheet, frame, collage, full illustration, complex character design, elaborate ornament, thin sketching, dense hatching, or realistic painted rendering.
- Never add words, letters, numbers, captions, labels, logos, watermarks, or signatures.
